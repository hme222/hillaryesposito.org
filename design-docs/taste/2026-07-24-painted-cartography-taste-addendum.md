# Taste Addendum: Painted Cartography

Date: 2026-07-24  
Reference: user-provided “Risograph Cartography” screenshot

## Reference analysis

### What makes it feel good

- Cartography behaves as image-making, not wayfinding.
- Botanical photography, watery pigment, photocopy distortion, drawn contours, and narrow vertical fragments coexist without resolving into one literal map.
- The page feels assembled by hand: overlaps are imperfect, edges interrupt one another, and some source material is partially erased.
- White paper and negative space keep the collage from becoming visually heavy.

### What to borrow

- Maps as source material rather than interface.
- Organic photography mixed with technical drawing.
- Watercolor bloom, uneven ink density, halftone, and torn plate edges.
- A few vertical stretched fragments that create rhythm.
- Portrait integrated into the artwork instead of framed as a conventional card.

### What to avoid

- Latitude/longitude ticks, compass, scale bar, graticule, plotted route, registration targets, and survey language.
- Military or GIS associations.
- A collage so dense that the portfolio promise becomes difficult to scan.
- Copying the reference composition or artwork.

## Taste evolution

The quiet-authority foundation remains. The correction is within the imagery layer:

- **Previous interpretation:** precise cartography with Riso texture.
- **Preferred interpretation:** painterly mixed-media collage that happens to contain cartographic fragments.
- **Strong anti-pattern:** land-navigation visual language.

## Craft standard

- Decorative art remains behind a stable reading clearing.
- Left-side negative space protects headline readability.
- Organic photography and pigment carry more visual weight than map instrumentation.
- Technical linework is allowed only as a partial texture.
- Light and dark modes must both retain the sense of layered print.

## Editorial translation

The target is an art-book spread, not a moodboard:

- sharp paper panels and thin rules establish the page;
- small caption strips behave like folios and image credits;
- case-study progress is horizontal and typographic, never a plotted vertical route;
- selected work is a ruled index rather than a stack of elevated cards;
- metrics read as an evidence column with shared rules rather than independent tiles;
- project color editions tint one shared painterly plate without erasing project identity.
