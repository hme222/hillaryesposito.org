# Advocate B argues for: The Page Remembers

**Role:** motion-designer · Advocate B  
**Date:** 2026-08-11  
**Status:** Debate proposal only — no app code, media generation, or credit spend authorised  
**Evidence status:** Portfolio claims and artifacts are verified from the repository. Higgsfield capability notes are verified from current official product/academy pages. The proposed choreography, performance budget, and comprehension thresholds are practitioner synthesis and require testing.

## The argument

The portfolio should spend its one surprising moment on a transition the visitor already asked for: opening the work. On activation, the exact artifact in a homepage project row does not disappear and reappear on another page. It remains optically continuous while the Riso paper around it performs one brief impossible change, then resolves into the case-study hero. The artifact is the fixed point; the publication transforms around it.

This makes Higgsfield felt as editorial continuity rather than presented as content. There is no new homepage module, no invitation to operate an AI effect, no movie to watch, and no method to understand. A recruiter still sees the current proof, project title, description, and ordinary case-study link. The richer transition happens only after that link is activated, during navigation the visitor already initiated, and it yields instantly to the destination.

The concept is ambitious because it treats two separately designed pages as one continuous physical edit. The surprise is not “an image moved.” It is that the same trustworthy fragment seems to survive a change in scale, paper, light, crop, and editorial context without its pixels ever changing.

## One-sentence visitor experience

**I choose a project, its real artifact stays under my eye, and the entire publication changes around it until I am standing inside that case study.**

## Exact homepage teaser

The current `Selected work` section remains the teaser. It receives no new heading, framework name, explainer, selector, play control, or disclosure.

### At rest

Preserve the existing three linked rows and their current recruiter-readable content:

1. `01 · Memorial Sloan Kettering` with the existing subtitle, description, `mskcc-map-thumb.jpg`, and route `/case-study/msk`.
2. `02 · Grove` with the existing subtitle, description, `grove1.png`, visible `Active · Phase 2 of 3` status, and route `/case-study/grove`.
3. `03 · Mobbin` with the existing subtitle, description, `discover.jpg`, and route `/case-study/mobbin`.

No motion plays at rest, on scroll, or merely because a pointer crosses a row. Focus and hover keep the existing restrained treatment.

### On activation

1. **0 ms — route wins:** clicking, tapping, pressing Enter, or voice-activating the project link initiates navigation immediately. The browser URL and destination render are never held for a cinematic cue.
2. **0–120 ms — continuity lock:** a deterministic duplicate of the exact thumbnail occupies the thumbnail's measured bounds. The selected row receives the normal pressed/focus response; all text remains readable until the destination takes over.
3. **120–560 ms — impossible splice:** a short, silent, source-free Higgsfield plate appears *behind* the locked artifact. Paper grain changes direction, one ink impression loosens into a dry pigment edge, and the case-study edition's light arrives from the gutter. The move is a shallow optical handoff, not a pan through a place: maximum apparent translation 24 px, no rotation, no zoom through depth, no loop.
4. **360–680 ms — destination resolves:** the exact same protected artifact is deterministically re-cropped/repositioned into its case-study hero or first evidence position. Destination type and navigation cross-fade in. The generated plate is already leaving by `560 ms`; it must never obscure a title, status, qualifier, or control.
5. **By 680 ms — done:** the clip is removed from the render tree. Focus lands according to the existing SPA route contract. If the destination is ready sooner, the transition may finish sooner; it never makes the recruiter wait for the media.

There is no teaser copy about Higgsfield. If production credit is required, it belongs in a concise case-study making note, for example: `Transition atmosphere made with Higgsfield; product and evidence imagery remains unaltered.`

## The three project expressions

### Mobbin — the crop finds its sequence

- **Protected carry-through:** the exact Discover source crop remains unchanged and attributed; Kikoff, Polymarket, Discover, and Mobbin ownership stay visible in the destination content.
- **Source-free action:** blank contact-paper exposure marks appear in three beats around the crop, then the surrounding paper changes from a single aperture to a three-aperture rhythm. No app pixel is regenerated, and the effect never pretends Hillary designed the source products.
- **Deterministic landing:** the Discover image becomes its exact place in the existing three-source hero. Kikoff and Polymarket enter as ordinary DOM/image layers, not generated reveals.
- **Smaller case-study echo:** when `Open the capture desk` is activated, the three exact source crops receive a simultaneous 160 ms opacity settle while their existing labels remain continuously visible. No cascade; sequence is expressed by the page's real structure.

## Why Higgsfield matters here

Use Higgsfield only for the otherwise expensive, source-free in-between material: paper behavior, pigment edge, botanical light, exposure falloff, and optical continuity. The current official workflow supports the relevant controls:

- **Start and end frames:** models that expose both slots animate between specified endpoints rather than inventing the entire destination. This is appropriate for a tightly art-directed transition plate. See [Higgsfield Academy: Video generation](https://higgsfield.ai/academy/courses/cinema-studio-complete-tour/video-generation).
- **First/last-image reference and motion control:** lock the visual endpoints and use a reference animatic to prescribe pace. See [Higgsfield Motion Control](https://higgsfield.ai/create/motion-control).
- **Cinema Studio camera/optical control:** use a static or extremely shallow multi-axis move, controlled lens response, and restrained texture rather than the platform's high-energy presets. Higgsfield describes camera, lens, motion, and visual-reference controls on the same official Motion Control page.
- **Short low-resolution blocking:** the official academy explicitly recommends blocking short and at lower resolution before finalising. That makes the proposed proof sequence credit-conscious.

Do **not** use Canvas to rewrite, clean, relight, or replace any artifact pixel, even though the product can do so. The capability is technically available but conflicts with this portfolio's truth boundary. Do not use avatars, sound, lipsync, text generation, product placement, or an Explainer workflow.

### Generation specification

- Generate each transition as a **blank-center/source-free plate** with a stable matte-safe aperture where the protected artifact will sit later.
- Feed start/end frames containing only paper, pigment, lighting, and a neutral registration proxy—never a product screenshot, map, workflow, logo, label, metric, or status.
- Use a rough deterministic reference animatic for motion control: `0–120 ms hold`, `120–420 ms transformation`, `420–560 ms settle`, then a clean tail for the cross-fade.
- Camera: locked or shallow lateral optical shift only; no orbit, parallax field, dolly-through, spin, whip, large-scale zoom, or speed ramp.
- Sound: off. Loop: off. Generated text: none.
- Produce separate desktop and mobile compositions; never crop a desktop master into portrait.

## Generative and deterministic boundary

| Layer | Source | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Project title, subtitle, description, status, link, focus, and route | Live DOM | Deterministic and always present; never baked into media |
| MSK workflow/map evidence and all claims | Repository artifact/component | Exact; never uploaded to or altered by a generator |
| Grove prototype screens, survey findings, overrides, and Phase 2 status | Repository artifact/DOM | Exact; never uploaded to or altered by a generator |
| Mobbin source-product captures, names, attribution, and contribution boundary | Repository artifact/DOM | Exact; never uploaded to or altered by a generator |
| Shared-element crop, size, position, clipping, and destination alignment | CSS/View Transitions or Motion | Deterministic; geometry is computed from live elements |
| Paper fibers, dry pigment edges, botanical shadow, exposure falloff, optical texture | Higgsfield output | Source-free atmosphere only; silent, non-semantic, removable |
| Final composite | Browser or editor | Protected artifact is placed above the generated plate at 100% fidelity; no generative pixels cross its matte |

The artifact layer must be inspectably identical before and after compositing. A pixel-diff check outside expected deterministic scaling/cropping is a release gate, not a visual preference.

## Replacement safety

The system binds the transition to a semantic slot (`project id`, `artifact id`, `aspect-ratio`, and destination anchor), not to image content. Generated plates contain no image-specific edges, wording, silhouettes, or shadows that need to “recognise” the current artifact.

- A same-ratio replacement drops into the deterministic artifact layer with no regeneration.
- A different-ratio replacement uses `object-fit: contain` inside the safe aperture and the case study's authored crop rules; blank paper absorbs the difference.
- If the new artifact cannot remain legible or truthful in the existing slot, the transition turns off for that project until its two deterministic layouts are recomposed. Navigation and the destination remain complete.
- Replacing Grove's homepage image does not move `grove-live-care.jpg` out of case-study context unless Hillary separately approves that source change.
- CI should fail on missing source, missing destination anchor, a source checksum mismatch against an approved manifest, nonzero protected-region pixel diff, or a transition longer than the budget.

This is stronger replacement safety than a generated scene: no chair, wall, hand, frame, device, surface, or lighting contact has to be regenerated when the artifact changes.

## Adaptive equivalents

### Mobile and touch

- The entire project row remains the 44 px-or-larger link target; there is no precision gesture.
- Use a separately composed portrait plate and a 320–420 ms maximum handoff. The artifact moves no more than 12 px and stays within the viewport; the source-free paper change happens mainly through opacity and `clip-path`/matte reveal.
- Do not force the destination hero into a desktop composition. If the hero anchor is below the fold, land the artifact at the first truthful mobile evidence position or end the continuity at the header edge.
- Orientation change, browser back, a second activation, or an interrupted navigation cancels the plate and shows the destination immediately.

### Reduced motion

- No generated clip loads or plays.
- Keep the route change immediate; use a simultaneous 120 ms opacity cross-fade at most, with no travel, scale, stagger, blur, or parallax.
- Preserve the relationship with a repeated exact thumbnail and matching project title, not movement.

### Low data / Save-Data / constrained connection

- Do not request transition video. Use a 12–20 KB static source-free texture already available in the portfolio or no atmosphere at all.
- If a lightweight bridge is retained, limit it to a 160 ms CSS opacity change using composited properties. No blank loading state and no “download animation” prompt.

### No media, failed media, no JavaScript, or unsupported View Transitions

- Follow the link normally. The current project row and full case-study page are the complete experience.
- Do not show an error, substitute invented imagery, or reserve an empty transition stage.
- Browser back returns to the same project row through the existing history/focus behavior; it does not reverse-play the clip.

### Screen reader, keyboard, switch, voice, zoom, and forced colours

- Treat the transition layer as `aria-hidden` and unfocusable. Announce only the existing route/page title behavior; do not announce “animation playing.”
- The activated link remains a real link. Enter, touch, switch activation, and voice command use the same route and may receive the same nonessential visual handoff.
- At 200%/400% zoom and forced colours, omit the media and use ordinary page rendering. No meaning is carried by paper, colour, exposure, or motion.

## Performance and implementation guardrails

- Generated atmosphere is progressive enhancement and may not enter LCP for the homepage. Do not preload three videos.
- On a capable connection, prefetch only the focused/hovered project's small plate after a 150 ms intent delay; on touch, rely on an already cached poster or skip the clip. Never delay navigation to fetch it.
- Target per project/format: AV1/WebM ≤220 KB, H.264 fallback ≤300 KB, silent, 24 fps, 560 ms useful duration with a 120 ms clean hold. Hard ceiling: 400 KB per requested asset.
- Decode only after explicit project activation or clear intent; remove the video/canvas immediately after completion.
- Deterministic motion uses `transform`, `opacity`, and a compositor-safe mask where supported. Avoid animating layout, `filter` blur, large paint areas, or persistent `will-change`.
- Maintain a strict 16.7 ms frame budget on a mid-range mobile profile. If decode is late, GPU context is lost, long-task time exceeds 50 ms, or two frames miss, cut to the destination.
- Never trap focus, intercept Escape, disable scrolling after landing, or block a second navigation attempt.

## Lowest-cost proof test

### Gate 0 — prove the interaction without Higgsfield credits

Build a disposable route-transition prototype outside production using the current three homepage images, screenshots of the current destination heroes, two hand-made blank paper stills, and CSS/Web Animations. No app integration and no generated media. The stills cross-fade behind an exact shared artifact in **560 ms**.

Test with five time-boxed hiring-audience participants, including one keyboard-only and one motion-sensitive participant:

1. Give each person ten seconds on the homepage.
2. Ask them to open one project naturally.
3. After landing, ask: `What project did you open? What stayed the same? What, if anything, seemed altered or fabricated? Where would you go next for proof?`
4. Compare against the current instant route change, not against a poster or film.

Proceed only if all are true:

- 5/5 reach the selected case study without instruction.
- 5/5 correctly name the project; at least 4/5 correctly identify the carried artifact as the same source.
- 0/5 believe a product screen, result, metric, or shipped state was generated or changed.
- At least 4/5 describe the transition as deliberate/connected rather than “an ad,” “a loading animation,” or “an AI demo.”
- The motion-sensitive participant reports no discomfort and can use the reduced version without loss.
- Median route-to-readable-title is no slower than the current baseline by more than 100 ms.

### Gate 1 — one cheap generation

Only after Gate 0 passes, generate **MSK only** at low resolution with a 2–3 second source clip, then trim its usable middle to the 560 ms transition. Use one start frame, one end frame, and one motion-reference animatic. Cap exploration at **four low-resolution attempts and one refinement**; record the actual credit cost because current cost varies by model, duration, and account. If a clean source-free result does not appear within the cap, stop—the idea has failed cheaply.

Run protected-region pixel diff, 6× slow-motion timing inspection, desktop/mobile decode tests, Save-Data/no-media fallback checks, and the same five-person wording. Generate Grove and Mobbin only after MSK clears those gates.

## Why this cannot be mistaken for the rejected work

- It adds **no homepage object**. The current work index remains the interface.
- It has **no name on the homepage**, no headline, no framework, no decision copy, no endpoints, no range, no buttons beyond the existing project link, and no expandable AI note.
- It asks for **zero exploratory interaction**. The visitor does not select a state, scrub a transformation, compare before/after, inspect a table, or learn a method.
- It is **not a poster**: there is no self-contained rectangle combining headline, artifact, controls, and CTA. The visual event crosses two routes and then disappears.
- It is **not a film or player**: there is no play surface, timeline, pause, replay, captions, or viewing task. The sub-second source-free plate is transition material triggered by navigation.
- It is **not a dashboard or filter UI**: no data display or choice architecture is added.
- It is **not an environmental/spatial scene**: there is no room, landscape, pedestal, studio, museum, desk, device tableau, or world around the work. Paper/light changes are abstract transition matter, not a place.
- It does not duplicate or summarize evidence. It carries one exact artifact into the page where the evidence already lives.

The rejected Frame of Intent made the visitor look at the system around the work. The Page Remembers makes the system vanish at the moment the visitor chooses the work.

## Who this serves best

- **Time-boxed recruiters:** the selected project and ordinary route remain immediate; the moment improves orientation without adding reading.
- **Product and design leaders:** continuity demonstrates interaction judgment, transition architecture, restraint, and truth boundaries in one action.
- **AI-product leaders and AI-sceptical visitors:** the generative contribution is technically ambitious but visibly subordinate to unchanged evidence.
- **Keyboard, touch, switch, and voice users:** the existing semantic link is the only action.
- **Low-data, no-media, zoom, forced-colour, and reduced-motion visitors:** the complete route and meaning survive with a quieter continuity cue or none.

## Who it serves least

- Visitors who never open a case study will not see the primary Higgsfield moment. That is intentional: the work should earn attention before the effect does.
- People expecting a showreel or obvious demonstration of generative-video range will find it too restrained.
- Very old browsers and aggressively constrained devices receive the ordinary route change, so the signature craft is not universally visible even though the portfolio remains complete.
- A visitor who interprets any transition as latency may prefer the no-media equivalent; performance testing must be unforgiving.
- Screen-reader-only visitors receive the semantic relationship through stable link/page naming, not an equivalent “wow” effect. The concept is accessible in task and meaning, but its optical surprise is inherently visual.

## Trade-offs

This direction concentrates effort in route choreography rather than giving the homepage a persistent showpiece. It depends on exact destination anchors and careful responsive composition. Its generated layer must be aggressively edited; a merely competent result will look like a loading transition, and a flamboyant result will look like an AI reel. The production win is also its strategic limitation: because the AI stays almost invisible, Higgsfield itself will not be legible without a secondary making note.

That is the correct trade. The brief asks for Hillary's judgment to register first.

## Strongest rebuttal to the likely environmental concept

An environmental concept can create a beautiful world around the artifacts, and its strongest argument is emotional immersion: one authored space could make the portfolio feel expansive, cinematic, and immediately unlike a normal case-study index. I concede that it may produce the faster screenshot-level “wow.”

But that strength is also its structural weakness. A room, landscape, exhibition, desk, or atmospheric set is a second subject competing with Hillary's work. It forces real evidence to become scenery, a prop, or a small surface inside an invented world; replacement then means re-staging light, perspective, occlusion, and contact. Even a refined environment can be mistaken for generic AI art direction because the visitor has no reason to connect its spectacle to the act of evaluating product judgment.

The Page Remembers makes the surprising transformation causal. It happens only because the visitor chose a project, it preserves the exact thing they chose, and it lands them where that evidence can be examined. The environmental concept says, “look at the world I can generate.” This direction says, “you asked for the work; the whole portfolio moved to keep it in your hand.” That is more specific to Hillary, more defensible to a sceptical hiring leader, safer when artifacts change, and materially closer to the brief's demand that AI disappear behind authored judgment.

## Debate position

Choose this direction if the goal is for a recruiter to feel one piece of unusually authored interaction craft **at the exact moment they commit to the work**, while every proof, route, qualifier, and fallback remains ordinary and dependable. Reject it if the desired outcome is a persistent homepage spectacle that communicates Higgsfield's range before the visitor chooses a project; this concept intentionally refuses that job.

## Cross-examination of The Work Has Weather

**Strongest point conceded:** A reaches the homepage-only recruiter whom B deliberately does not: without adding copy or a control, three project-specific atmospheric states could make the existing index memorable *before* anyone commits to a case study, and the unchanged rows preserve a faster path than any new showpiece would.

**Two dangerous failure modes:**

1. **Ambient motion becomes the protagonist or the cost of reading.** A asks a viewport-wide field to respond to scrolling/focus for up to 3.2 seconds per row. If it is strong enough to register, it can compete with three concise claims, create repeated full-field motion for vestibular-sensitive visitors, and consume decode/memory during a ten-second scan; if subdued enough to avoid those costs, it becomes expensive wallpaper. A still fallback solves access and data, but it does not prove the flagship version improves comprehension rather than preference.
2. **The environments imply meaning the artifacts cannot prove.** “Clinical corridors align,” “a greenhouse exhales,” and “an archive multiplies” are elegant but generic semantic theatre unless visitors connect each effect to the actual decision. MSK is especially exposed because the current homepage location-map crop does not evidence four systems becoming two; Mobbin is exposed because immersive depth can make third-party screens feel like Hillary-authored product work. Any artifact replacement also has to preserve the environment's perspective, intensity, and metaphor—not merely fit its safe zone—or the supposedly project-specific weather becomes interchangeable AI art direction.

**What would make me switch from B to A:** Run the same five hiring-side participants through randomized current/A/B prototypes with first-view cache and network conditions held constant. I would switch if A, without explanation, gets **at least 4/5** participants to identify all three projects as meaningfully different, **at least 4/5** to open a relevant case study within ten seconds, **0/5** to infer altered evidence or inflated ownership, and beats B by **at least two participants** on unaided recall of one real project decision after five minutes—while matching the current page within **100 ms** on readable-index timing, staying within its frame/weight budgets, and producing no discomfort in the motion-sensitive participant. I would also require the result to survive one deliberately mismatched replacement artifact without regeneration; if A remains specific and B's click-only moment yields no measurable recall or orientation advantage, A has earned the homepage.
