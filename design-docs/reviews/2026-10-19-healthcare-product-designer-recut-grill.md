_Date: 2026-10-19_

## The hiring decision this work must support

This portfolio is being recut for **Healthcare Product Designer** roles. It is not being optimized for a broad Senior Product Designer claim, a pure UXR role, or a service-design-only role.

The default recruiter path must answer, in order:

1. Can Hillary understand a consequential healthcare workflow?
2. Can she turn research and constraints into a product decision?
3. Can she design and prototype credible digital interactions?
4. Has she owned delivery under real operational stakes?
5. Is she precise about what shipped, what was tested, and what remains unproven?

## Confirmed facts

- MSK is the strongest healthcare workflow and stakeholder-alignment case. The proposed digital improvement was implemented after Hillary changed roles; the case must not imply that she owned the implementation or post-launch iteration.
- Grove includes a five-person moderated test, a 35-person survey, a twelve-to-three feature decision, a working coded prototype, and a pending retest. It is not a shipped mature product.

- Mobbin is client documentation, taxonomy, and retrieval work on third-party products; it is not product ownership.
- The recruiter panel already contains View résumé and Email me actions. The Home contact area currently needs a résumé action beside Email.
- The existing native `<details>` / `<summary>` pattern and `.rp-deepDive` styling already provide an accessible progressive-disclosure base.
- The portfolio does not yet prove a directly owned digital-product cycle through release, observed behavior, revision, and measured result.

## Recommendations and assumptions

- The recruiter panel should lead with MSK → Grove → Knowunity, then expose a plainly labeled supporting-evidence group containing Medical Logistics, UX Woman, and Mobbin. Medical Logistics also receives an always-visible Delivery Evidence treatment so “supporting” does not mean hidden.
- A reader should be able to understand each case without opening a disclosure. Disclosures contain depth, not missing logic.
- Default scan quality should be evaluated with a decision-trace checklist, not a fixed word-count or percentage-scroll target.
- Specific disclosure labels should preview the evidence inside. Generic “Learn more” text is rejected.
- A direct URL fragment targeting content inside a closed disclosure should open the enclosing disclosure, scroll normally to the target, and avoid unexpected focus movement.
- The Spanish experience remains behaviorally unchanged in this slice. Any later parity rewrite requires a separate content review.

## Pressure-test: strongest objections

### “Why not let UX Woman lead? It is healthcare research.”

Because it proves discovery discipline but not yet a tested product decision. Six interviews and five designed pretotypes are credible supporting evidence; zero pretotypes run makes it too early to anchor a product-design hiring claim.

### “Why not let Logistics lead? It has the best outcomes.”

Because a Healthcare Product Designer panel will still look for digital interaction, prototyping, and collaboration with engineering. Logistics is too valuable to bury, but it cannot carry that digital-product proof by itself. Its best role is a prominent ownership-and-delivery counterweight to the primary digital cases.

### “Why is Knowunity primary when it is untested?”

Because it supplies interaction craft and implementation evidence that the other cases do not show as directly. Its primary placement is conditional on keeping the prototype status, contribution split, mocked-recall boundary, and required student test visible by default.

### “Won’t disclosures look like hiding weak evidence?”

They will if role, status, decision, result, or limitations disappear behind them. The contract therefore defines a non-negotiable visible evidence spine. Only repetition, exhaustive galleries, extended methods, and secondary diagrams may collapse.

### “Can a recut solve the direct product-ownership gap?”

No. It can make the current evidence faster to evaluate and more accurately positioned. It cannot fabricate a shipped product loop. Grove or another real product still needs a documented test/release → observed failure → revision → measured result.

## Never-hide rule

Every default case-study path must keep these visible:

- role and contribution;
- shipped, implemented, tested, prototype, or research status;
- ownership boundaries;
- sample sizes when research is cited;
- metric attribution and qualification;
- strongest artifact;
- primary product or service decision;
- outcome or current state;
- any limitation that materially changes the claim.

## Rejected or deferred

- A global Highlights / Full toggle: rejected; it adds an unnecessary mode and state problem.
- Hiding entire case-study logic behind disclosures: rejected.
- Fixed “cut 40%” or scroll-height targets: rejected as a proxy that can reward deleting evidence.
- Recasting Medical Logistics as a conventional digital-product case: rejected as inaccurate.
- Promoting UX Woman before a pretotype run: deferred until evidence exists.
- Claiming broad senior product ownership: deferred until a directly owned digital iteration loop exists.
- New visual system, runtime dependency, or bespoke accordion component: out of scope.
- Spanish copy recut: deferred to a separate content and native-language review.
