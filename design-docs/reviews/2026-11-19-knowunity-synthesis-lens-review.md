# Knowunity visuals through the Shape of AI "Synthesis" lens (2026-11-19)

Lens: https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/synthesis. Synthesis combines many sources and restructures them into patterns or themes, which adds interpretation beyond a summary. Its four design rules: keep reasoning visible; separate evidence from interpretation; expose uncertainty; let the reader verify key conclusions. It names four kinds: aggregated, comparative, thematic, generative.

The pattern is written for AI products. Knowunity's prototype is a voice product, but this page is not one, so the lens is used as a trust test: the phone screens, the reel, the four decision steps, the two numbers and the badge are Hillary's own synthesis, and the four rules decide whether a reader can tell what was observed, what was built, what is faked, and what is only a guess. The study's limits: the speech listening is faked (hold length decides the result), and no student has tried it.

Study: Knowunity, `/case-study/knowunity` (`FlagshipKnowunity.tsx`, `DecisionStory.tsx`, `EvidenceField.tsx`, `CaseStudyCredential.tsx`, `knowunityBadge.ts`, Spanish data in `spanishCaseStudies.ts`).

Seen live at 1440 x 1000 and 390 x 844 (mobile, 2x): hero phone fan, Problem section with "What Knowunity asked for" open, "Knowunity gave me / I did" split, the 52-second reel and its "What the reel shows" list, the mock note under the buttons, "See how I built it" (open), the four decision steps (each step captured at desktop), outcomes with the badge. The Spanish page was loaded at 1440 because its visuals differ (no phone captures, no decision visual; badge and reel only). A "What I built" result line under the hero was being added by another agent during the review and is not judged.

## What already works

- **The biggest limit is in the hero, in bold, in the first paragraph.** "It can't hear or grade real speech yet, and no student has tried it." A reader cannot reach the phone screens without passing it, on desktop or phone.
- **The reel carries its own caveat.** The caption directly under the video says "The listening is faked: it can't hear or grade real speech yet." It is a caption on the visual, not a footnote elsewhere, which is what rule 2 asks for.
- **The faking mechanism is stated, not just admitted.** "In the prototype, how long you hold the mic decides the result. On question 2, the first try always 'mishears' you, so you can see that screen." A reader can check this themselves in the linked prototype, which is rule 4 done well.
- **Built versus given is split visually.** The "Knowunity gave me" / "I did" cards separate inputs (Knowie, Greed font, Figma kit, access to app and beta) from her work, and the "I did" card names Claude Code's role ("wrote most of the code from my written plans"). That is the "what was combined" part of rule 1.
- **Step 01 labels its guess as a guess.** "My guess, untested with students: seeing your own words proves you remember them." This is the one causal claim in the decisions, and it is marked as interpretation in the sentence itself.
- **The outcomes section states all limits beside the numbers.** The disclaimer line "Design-sprint prototype · the listening is faked · not tested with students · not part of the real Knowunity app" sits directly above the tiles, and the two numbers (43 pages, 2.5 weeks) are output and scope, tagged "Build" and "Sprint", not results dressed as impact. No Knowunity usage numbers or targets appear, matching the owner decision recorded in the source.
- **The badge can be verified.** "Verify on LinkedIn" sits under the badge, and the caption names the badge, program, partner and date. The step-to-screen pairing on desktop is accurate: each active step shows the screen it describes (pass card, recap with "Practice what I missed", mic primer, second-miss card).

## Gaps

1. **The phone screens show faked results as if they were real, with no label on the visual (high).** The hero's center phone and decision step 01 show "Nailed it — that's the whole definition", "You said: 'It's the charge on an atom if you split every bond's electrons evenly between the two atoms'", and "You said you were sure, and you were. +1 XP". The recap shows "You can say 3 of 4 back without help". These are scripted strings, not a student's words or a graded answer, but they read as an observed session. The hero text says the listening is faked; the screens themselves never do, and the decision panel at "I made the student do the proving" has no caveat near it at all. Fix: a short caption on the hero fan and on the decision visual using wording already on the page, e.g. the reel's "The listening is faked" plus "sample answer, scripted", so the caveat travels with the image as it does on the reel.
2. **"Knowunity reviewed it" is a headline claim with no visible evidence (high).** The outcomes title reads "Yummy Labs gave me a badge, and Knowunity reviewed it." The badge has a verify link; the review has nothing: no quote, no form (call, written notes, demo day), no outcome. The interview master file says "Knowunity's review is not quoted", so the page is asserting a review whose content the reader cannot see. By rule 3, an unverified claim placed in the headline reads as stronger than the badge, which is the verified one. Fix (needs Hillary's input): say what form the review took and that its content is not quoted, or move "Knowunity reviewed it" out of the title into the intro with that qualifier.
3. **The badge's visible caption drops "Completed" (medium).** The badge image itself says "Completed" above "Yummy Labs AI Design Sprint", and the alt text says "completed the Yummy Labs AI Design Sprint". The visible caption says "AI Design Sprinter badge · Yummy Labs AI Design Sprint, in partnership with Knowunity · Sept 2026", and the title says Yummy Labs "gave me a badge". A reader can take it as a judged award rather than a completion credential; the word that settles it is only legible in the image (rendered 156 px wide at 1440, so its text is a few pixels tall). Fix: add "completion" to the caption, using the badge's own word. If the badge was in fact judged, Hillary should say so; it is not on the page.
4. **On phones, three of the four decisions lose their evidence (medium).** Below 900 px the decision panel stacks and the visual is fixed to the first frame (`active === null` gives `STEP_FRAME[0]`), so at 390 px only the pass screen appears, after all four steps. The recap ("Practice what I missed"), the mic primer and the second-miss card are never shown next to steps 02 to 04. The mic primer screen does not appear anywhere on the phone page; the recap and second-miss appear only as cropped side phones in the hero fan, which are `aria-hidden`. Steps 02 to 04 are design claims a reader could check against a screen on desktop but not on a phone. Fix: on the stacked layout, render each step's screen under its own step (the four captures already exist), or a small four-up strip.
5. **Interpretation lines are styled as findings (medium).** Each step ends with a bold logline: "Proof, not praise", "The student chooses the second try", "No surprise permission pop-up", "Every miss says what comes next". Three describe what the build does and are fine. "Proof, not praise" restates the untested guess from step 01's body as a bold verdict, in the same style as the factual ones, so the "My guess, untested" qualifier is lost to anyone scanning. The section intro "each of these four changes makes it easier to keep going" is also an untested claim stated as fact. The Spanish page has an eyebrow "Cuatro decisiones que tomé, aún sin probar" ("four decisions I made, still untested"); the English page has no equivalent. Fix: bring the Spanish eyebrow's meaning to the English section as a kicker (an English line equal to the existing Spanish one), so every step reads as untested.
6. **Step 02's "used to" has no stated source (low-medium).** "A missed question used to come back by itself at the end." The reader cannot tell whether "used to" means Knowunity's live app, Knowunity's beta, or an earlier version of her own prototype. Those are different kinds of evidence (observed in their product versus her own iteration). Fix (needs Hillary's input): name which version it was, e.g. "In Knowunity's beta" or "In my first version", whichever is true.
7. **The reel's on-screen lines claim listening the caption denies (low).** The reel list reads "Talk it through. Knowie listens." and "Check what it heard." The caption right above the list says the listening is faked, so the conflict is resolved nearby; it is low because the caveat is adjacent. Fix: none needed in the video; keeping the caption directly under the player, as now, is the fix. Check it stays there if the layout changes.
8. **Knowunity's two goals are never closed (low).** "What Knowunity asked for" names two goals (more students start the new step; more who start, finish). Nothing later says neither was measured. The disclaimer "not tested with students" implies it, but a reader has to connect them. Fix: one clause in the outcomes intro saying the goals were not measured because no student used it, using facts already on the page.
9. **Text inside the phone captures is far below the label floor (medium).** The page has no SVG diagrams with text (0 SVGs at 1440; 1 decorative SVG with no text at 390), and all HTML text measured is at or above the 13 px `--ts-label` floor (smallest: 13 px kickers, reel note, card labels). The problem is raster text in the 780 x 1688 captures. Their rendered scale versus the app's 390 px CSS width: decision visual 248 px wide at 1440 (0.64x) and 228 px at 390 (0.58x); hero center phone 286 px at 1440 (0.73x) and 148 px at 390 (0.38x); hero side phones 349 / 181 px. Estimated from the screenshots, the smallest in-screen text ("You said", "Chemistry · 1/4", the recap's "You were sure about this one") renders at about 6 to 7 px in the desktop decision visual, about 5 to 6 px in the phone decision visual, and about 4 px on the phone hero. The quoted "You said" sentence, which is the evidence for step 01, renders at about 7 px on desktop. These are estimates from pixels, not measured font sizes, because the prototype source was not available. The alt text and step bodies carry the content, so nothing is lost to a screen reader. Fix: on the decision visual, a cropped close-up of the "You said" card for step 01 and the "One more go" card for step 04, at a size where the quoted text clears 13 px.
