# Medical logistics visuals through the Shape of AI "Synthesis" lens (2026-11-19)

Lens: https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/synthesis. Synthesis combines many sources and restructures them into patterns or themes, which adds interpretation beyond a summary. Its four design rules: keep reasoning visible; separate evidence from interpretation; expose uncertainty; let the reader verify key conclusions. It names four kinds: aggregated, comparative, thematic, generative.

The pattern is written for AI products. This page is not one, so the lens is used as a trust test. The move map, the three before/after drawings, the order-status board and the three outcome numbers are Hillary's own synthesis of her work. The same four rules decide whether a reader can tell what was measured, what comes only from her Officer Evaluation Report (OER), what is a schematic and what is her own reading of cause.

Study: Medical logistics (U.S. Army), `/case-study/logistics`. Sources read: `FlagshipLogistics.tsx`, `LogisticsMoveMap.tsx`, `LogisticsMechanism.tsx`, `flagship/OrderStatusBoard.tsx`, `EvidenceField.tsx`, `LOGISTICS_ES` in `spanishCaseStudies.ts`, and `design-docs/case-study-stories/logistics.md` (where the metrics' attribution was recorded).

Seen live at 1440 x 1000 and 390 x 844 (phone, 2x), all disclosures open, both Before/After switches toggled: hero move map (Before/After), "My job / The risk" band, three move cards with their drawings, order-status board (Before/After), "Three limits" (inside "See what made it hard"), outcomes. Spanish checked at 1440.

## What already works

- **The move map says what it is, on the visual.** The caption under it reads "A schematic, not a map: no real places, routes or unit positions." It is at 13 px, the site floor, and it sits on the figure, not in a footnote. The legend counts match the text: "Aid station (7)", "Country (3)".
- **The map is a fair comparative synthesis.** Before and After keep the same seven stations and three zones; only the supply point and its route lengths change. The After view leaves a dashed ghost of the old supply point with a line to the new one, so the reader can see the difference without toggling back. That is the "visual diff" the lens asks for.
- **The order-status board is labeled as a reconstruction.** "Recreated to explain the idea. Not the original system, and no real data." sits at the top left of the board at 13 px, before the rows. The Before state does not overclaim: six rows say only "Status not visible", one says "Out of supply, found late".
- **The hero names its source.** "Restocking then took 1–2 weeks, according to my Officer Evaluation Report, the Army's written review of my work." It also explains what an OER is, and it uses "then", a time order, not "because".
- **The page leads with the range, not the percentage.** The lead metric tile shows "1–2 wk" at 128 px; "85% faster" is a short tail on its label. This follows the 2026-10-15 decision in the stories file.
- **Uncertainty is stated above the numbers, not under them.** "All three numbers come from my Officer Evaluation Report. Records of how and over what time they were measured were not kept." It sits between the outcomes title and the tiles, so a reader meets the limit before the numbers. This is rule 3 met in words.
- **Spanish keeps the limit closer to the claim.** The Spanish page has no map, drawings or board (it renders as text), so the visual gaps below do not apply. Its intro carries "No se guardaron registros de cómo ni durante qué periodo se midió" right after the 1–2 semanas claim, which the English hero does not.

## Gaps

1. **The cause behind each number is styled as part of the evidence (high).** On the outcomes, each tile carries a small tag under its label: "Warehouse move", "Order tracker", "Shared process". On MSK the same tag slot names the kind of claim (Led, Trained, Initiative result, Projection). Here it names the cause, in the same voice as the number. The labels do the same: the 60% tile says "once stations could see their orders and stopped ordering too much or running out". The disclaimer says the numbers come from the OER. It does not say who tied each number to one change. The stories file (Q5 and the 2026-09-27 follow-up) shows that the one-to-one pairing is Hillary's own attribution: "this figure belongs specifically to the warehouse relocation", "order-status visibility earned the 60%". That is a credible first-hand reading, but it is interpretation, and the three changes overlapped in the same deployment. Fix: keep the tag slot for the kind of claim, as on MSK (for example "From my OER"), and move cause into a plainly marked line, such as "My read: this came from the order tracker." **Needs Hillary's input:** does the OER itself tie each number to a change, or only list them? The wording depends on the answer.

2. **"85% faster" is more precise than the range it summarizes, and its source is unclear (high).** The lead tile reads "down from 1–2 months · 85% faster". The stated range does not support one figure. Using about 4.3 weeks a month, 1–2 months down to 1–2 weeks is anywhere from about 53% faster (1 month to 2 weeks) to about 88% faster (2 months to 1 week). The sources also conflict. The comment at the top of `FlagshipLogistics.tsx` says the 85% is "in the Officer Evaluation Report". The 2026-10-15 decision in `case-study-stories/logistics.md` says "the service record supports '1–2 months to 1–2 weeks'; the 85% is a summary of that range". It also says the 85% would appear as a small line marked "(owner-reported)". On the live tile it has no attribution of its own; it inherits the OER disclaimer. Fix, depending on Hillary's answer: if the OER states 85%, mark it as the OER's figure on the tile. If she worked it out from the range, either drop it from the tile or show it as her estimate. **Needs Hillary's input:** which source the 85% comes from.

3. **The 15% has no interview record and its pairing is inconsistent (medium-high).** The 15% tile reads "more efficient delivery of critical supplies after we set up one shared way to communicate", tagged "Shared process". In the stories file, Q5 gives the third chapter "No isolated percentage". The 15% and its tie to a communication protocol appear only in the page comment, which cites the résumé. "More efficient delivery" also gives no unit: time, cost or errors. Move card 02 calls the change "one shared way to order", but the tile calls it "one shared way to communicate". A reader cannot tell if they are the same change. Fix: **needs Hillary's input** on what the 15% measured and which change it belongs to. Until then, keep the number, drop the "Shared process" cause tag (see gap 1), and use the same name for the change on card 02 and on the tile.

4. **The move 03 drawing turns the 60% into a causal arrow and drops its limits (medium).** In card 03 ("Let every station track its orders"), the After row shows seven unlabeled green bars, then an arrow to "60% less spend". The arrow says the tracker caused the 60% (gap 1, drawn). The drawing also drops "with supplies still on hand" and the OER source, both of which are on the outcomes tile. The bars look like data, but no label or caption says what they are. The component is named `Forecasting` and draws consumption-style bars. The stories file says forecasting was real work but "stays out of public copy" until it is interviewed, and that the 60% belongs to status visibility, not forecasting. The bars bring back the image that 2026-09-27 fix removed from the words. Fix: replace the bars with something drawn from the board right below, such as a status row ("In transit", "Running low: order now"), or label them as illustration. Either drop the number from the drawing or mark it the same way as the tile. Card 01's "months → weeks" is a fair summary of the hero claim and can stay.

5. **The three small drawings carry no "schematic" cue (low-medium).** The map and the board each say what they are. The three move-card drawings do not. They are `aria-hidden` and have no caption, and the warehouse-move one ("BEFORE · SUPPLY POINT FAR BACK" to "AFTER · MOVED FORWARD") shows a distance cut by about two-thirds with no scale. A reader can take the line lengths for real proportions. Fix: one shared caption under the three cards, reusing the map's wording ("Schematics, not maps or real systems").

6. **"Real-time" is stronger than the text (low).** Card 03's drawing says "AFTER · SHARED REAL-TIME STATUS"; the text under it says the tracker "showed every station its orders as their status changed". The text does not say "real-time", and the stories file uses it only as Hillary's own word. Fix: use the card text's wording in the drawing, or confirm with Hillary that "real-time" is accurate.

7. **The headline states cause the body only implies (low).** The H1 says "cutting resupply from months to weeks". The sub under it is careful ("Restocking then took 1–2 weeks, according to my Officer Evaluation Report"). The "1–2 weeks / to resupply, after the move" readout at the top right of the map is careful too, but the OER source is only in the map's screen-reader label, not on the visual. Fix: add "per my OER" to the map readout, the way the aria-label already does. The H1 is a positioning line and can stand if gap 1 is fixed lower on the page.

8. **Drawing labels are below the site's 13 px floor (medium).** The smallest rendered SVG text, measured as font size times `getScreenCTM().a`:
   - 1440: the warehouse-move drawing's "BEFORE · SUPPLY POINT FAR BACK" / "AFTER · MOVED FORWARD" at 8.9 px; its "supply", "aid", "months → weeks" at 10.7 px; the other two drawings' BEFORE/AFTER headers at 12.2 px. Only "3 countries, one format", "stockout" and "60% less spend" reach 14.7 px.
   - 390: the warehouse-move headers at 8.0 px; the other drawings' headers at 8.2 px; every other label at 9.6–9.8 px. Nothing in the three drawings reaches 13 px on a phone.
   - The move map has no SVG text; its readout, legend and caption are HTML at 15 / 13 / 13 px at both widths. The board note and the outcomes disclaimer are 13 px. All pass.
   The comment in `LogisticsMechanism.tsx` says labels are about 9.6 px at 390, so the shortfall is already known. The card text beside each drawing carries the same content, so nothing is lost to a phone reader, only made harder to read. Fix: raise the label sizes, or shorten the BEFORE/AFTER headers so they can be set larger. Card 01 renders smaller than 02 and 03 at 1440 because it is wider and its viewBox scales differently; give it the same scale.
