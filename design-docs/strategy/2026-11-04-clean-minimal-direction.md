## Taste profile

**Emotional target:** calm and exact. A reader finishes a case knowing what changed and how she knows, without having to decode the page.

**Quality level:** flagship. Every element has a job, and anything without one is removed.

**References (from earlier sessions):** jakub.kr, alexwidua.com, cali.so, lfs.gd. What they share: very short pages, type doing most of the work, one small moment of motion, and almost no framing. What to borrow is the restraint. Do not borrow their lack of a result, because recruiters need one.

**Anti-references:** this site as it is now, per the instructor: many boxes, labels above every block, the same claim in several places, and a texture or a decorative image behind text.

## Principles

1. **One claim per section, said once.** Each section opens with its claim in plain words. The result appears in the hero and in the one section that proves it, not in five places. *Test:* search a page for its main number. It should appear at most twice, each time for a different reason.
2. **Type is the structure.** Two faces, a small scale (13, 15, 16, 18, 24, 40), hairlines for rules, no boxes inside boxes. *Test:* take away every border and fill. The page should still read in order.
3. **Fun answers a click.** Each page keeps one interaction that shows a change: the MSK filing queue, Grove's twelve to three, the Logistics before and after, the UX Woman confidence map. Scroll reveals stay small. *Test:* the page is fully readable with motion off, and each interaction teaches something that text can't.
4. **Chrome earns its place.** A label, strip or band stays only if removing it would cost a reader something. *Test:* for each piece of chrome, name the question it answers. If there isn't one, delete it.
5. **Plain words, short pages.** A case reads in about three minutes. *Test:* a reader with no healthcare background can say what changed after one pass.
