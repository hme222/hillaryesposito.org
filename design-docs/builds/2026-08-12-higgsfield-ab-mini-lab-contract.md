## Purpose

Give Hillary a fair, moving comparison of three Higgsfield experience grammars before selecting a direction or spending credits. The lab tests interaction and motion—not final material quality.

## Private surface

Create an unlinked, `noindex` route at `/lab/higgsfield-abc`. It will not appear on the public homepage, navigation, sitemap, or case studies.

The lab uses one real MSK work row and one existing case-study destination crop so all three directions receive identical evidence and comparable production effort.

## Fair-comparison protocol

Each mini receives the same first-look contract:

- one authentic MSK artifact;
- one primary visitor action;
- one complete 8–12 second experience;
- one sentence of guidance;
- one coherent still and reduced-motion equivalent;
- no more than one primary and one secondary control during the first-look path.

Advanced recovery scenarios for C are tested separately and do not appear during the initial A/B/C preference comparison.

## Mini A — The Work Has Weather

- Show the authentic MSK row in its normal editorial structure.
- Use locally simulated blank paper, pigment, shadow, and shallow camera movement around the unchanged artifact.
- No copy, artifact, or route changes.
- Primary action: `Reveal atmosphere`. Secondary action after completion: `Replay`.
- Complete the motion in 8–12 seconds. Provide a still-first state carrying the same composition and meaning.
- The simulation must not use a four-to-two metaphor unless the foreground is the authentic workflow artifact that proves it.

## Mini B — The Page Remembers

- Begin with the same authentic MSK row.
- Primary action: `Open project`. Preserve that exact artifact optically while the surrounding publication transitions into a simplified case-study destination.
- Use locally simulated blank paper/pigment material only.
- Complete the transition in 8–12 seconds. Secondary action after completion: `Replay`.
- The source and destination must use the same approved source file; if no truthful mapping exists, the prototype ends continuity at the case-study header edge rather than pretending the artifact became workflow evidence.

## Mini C — Spatial Intent Playground

This is a private experimental control derived from the useful systems premise beneath Frame of Intent. It is **not a revival of the rejected homepage module**.

- Begin with the same authentic MSK evidence used by A and B.
- Give the visitor one concrete production job: **create a cinematic treatment around this exact MSK artifact without changing the artifact**.
- Place it as a rigid, protected plane inside a compact spatial field. The field is the interface: it must not be surrounded by a settings panel or infrastructure diagram.
- Primary action: move one material anchor to shape framing, depth, emphasis, and atmosphere. Spatial input communicates only spatial qualities—never latency, consistency, cost, or willingness to wait.
- Provide equivalent named step controls for keyboard, switch, voice, and non-drag operation. Equivalent inputs resolve to the same canonical spatial values; pointer noise, timestamps, focus order, and motion preferences are excluded.
- Translate the spatial state into an internal intent envelope containing protected evidence bounds and permitted material changes. Do not expose it as a form.
- Direct manipulation changes only locally simulated source-free paper, shadow, crop clearing, registration tension, light, and camera treatment around the evidence.
- The evidence pixels, workflow words, role boundary, and provenance never warp, regenerate, disappear, or change meaning.
- Local processing supplies the immediate, reversible preview. After shaping, present one familiar commitment choice: `Preview now` or `Finish with highest fidelity`. This communicates the outcome/time tradeoff; the system recommends and explains the simulated route instead of asking the visitor to select infrastructure.
- Route explanation stays plain and contextual: `The preview stays on this device. The atmosphere can finish remotely in about 8 seconds.` Protected evidence always remains local and immutable. The prototype makes no network call and spends no Higgsfield credits.
- The first-look journey contains four beats only:
  1. **Shape locally** — immediate, reversible spatial preview.
  2. **Commit** — choose speed or fidelity; the protected artifact locks visibly without being enclosed in a frame.
  3. **Continue while it resolves** — local shaping remains usable during the simulated remote pass; further changes begin a clearly marked next draft and never mutate the committed intent or its receipt. Status is bounded, cancellable, and secondary.
  4. **Result + receipt** — surrounding material settles around the unchanged artifact and compact proof appears.
- Make execution idempotent. Each committed intent receives a stable intent ID derived from canonical spatial values, protected source version, permitted transformations, and fidelity choice. Repeating the same commit replays or retrieves the same simulated result instead of creating another run, charge, or visual duplicate.
- Return a compact receipt answering three questions only: `What stayed protected?`, `What changed?`, and `New or replayed?` Technical intent ID and processing route remain in an evaluator-only disclosure. The receipt stays visually secondary to the artifact and never becomes a dashboard.
- The default comparison demonstrates `local preview`, `ready`, `finishing`, and `complete`. Load, latency variation, inconsistency, duplicate commit, cancellation, and cloud unavailability live in a separate evaluator scenario drawer available only after the first-look comparison. Every scenario retains the spatial intent and offers a clear next action.
- Translate distributed-system behavior into UX inside evaluator scenarios:
  - **Load:** preserve the local preview and offer an honest faster/lower-craft result or a longer high-fidelity finish.
  - **Latency:** keep local shaping responsive, show a bounded expectation, and permit cancellation without losing the composition.
  - **Consistency:** keep exact evidence fixed while only surrounding material is marked `settling` and reconciled.
  - **Cloud unavailable:** retain the local result and offer `Try finish again`; never discard or block the work.
- Remove the rejected public-facing system name, oversized headline, duplicated evidence rail, production-run disclosure, instructional essay, and homepage placement. Inside the lab, label it only `C · Playground`.
- Use one short instruction: `Shape the atmosphere. The evidence stays exact.`
- Default state must already be coherent; play cannot be required to understand the project.
- During first look, expose only the fidelity choice and contextual `Cancel`; place `Reset` and `View receipt` after completion. The comparison shell owns replay and reduced-motion controls. No camera or hand tracking.
- Feedback is immediate for local actions, announced through a concise live region for asynchronous states, and never communicated through colour or motion alone. Reduced motion swaps spatial travel and layer settling for discrete state changes.
- Complete the first-look path in 8–12 seconds from the first action. The accessible equivalent preserves focus, canonical spatial values, and output identity at 200% zoom and across input methods.

The purpose of C is diagnostic: determine whether one simple embodied production task can make distributed AI execution understandable without explaining the machinery. If it still feels like a tool demo, control panel, infrastructure visualizer, or evidence trapped inside a frame, retire the interaction premise permanently.
