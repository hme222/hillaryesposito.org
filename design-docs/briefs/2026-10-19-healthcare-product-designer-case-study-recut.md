# Healthcare Product Designer Case-Study Recut — Design Brief

_Date: 2026-10-19_  
_Owner: Hillary_  
_Status: Approved 2026-10-19_  
_Target role: Healthcare Product Designer_

## Problem

The portfolio has credible healthcare, research, systems, and implementation evidence, but it asks a recruiter to read too much before the hierarchy becomes clear. Six public cases use similar narrative weight, evidence boundaries repeat, and deeper artifacts compete with the product decision they are meant to support.

The result is not a lack of substance. It is a selection problem: the portfolio makes the reviewer reconstruct which work proves healthcare judgment, digital craft, research-to-product reasoning, and real delivery ownership.

## Objective

Create a fast, truthful hiring path for Healthcare Product Designer roles by:

- leading with the most relevant digital-product evidence;
- preserving Medical Logistics as prominent delivery proof;
- making every case complete in its default state;
- moving exhaustive supporting material into accessible, specifically labeled disclosures;
- keeping résumé and email actions adjacent and discoverable;
- preserving every claim boundary that affects hiring interpretation.

## Positioning

**Primary claim:** Hillary is a Healthcare Product Designer who turns complex clinical and operational workflows into clear product decisions, credible prototypes, and implementation-ready systems.

**Primary digital-product proof:**

1. **MSK** — healthcare systems and workflow judgment.
2. **Grove** — research-to-product decisions, AI trust, and a coded prototype.
3. **Knowunity** — interaction craft, component thinking, and implementation proof.

**Prominent delivery proof:**

- **Medical Logistics** — direct ownership, service-system change, and qualified operating outcomes.

**Supporting evidence:**

- **UX Woman** — active healthcare research with an explicit next experiment.
- **Mobbin** — taxonomy, documentation, and retrieval-system judgment.

This supersedes the old case-order implication that operator-trust positioning requires MSK and Logistics to function as co-primary digital cases. Operator trust remains a differentiator inside a Healthcare Product Designer story.

## Audience and evidence standard

The primary audience is healthcare and health-tech product-design recruiters and hiring managers evaluating workflow depth, research judgment, interaction craft, prototyping, engineering collaboration, and evidence integrity.

Recent health-product postings reinforce that combination: clinical workflow complexity and end-to-end product design (Onyx Bio), complete health-app flows and build QA (Nolla Health), clinician research and shipped UI (Lyrebird), observation plus product data and engineering delivery (Healthtech-1), and research/testing/iteration with strong visual craft (Hims & Hers).

The portfolio can support interviews for those roles. It must not pretend to close the remaining shipped-product iteration gap.

## Experience principles

### 1. Highlights are a complete story

No disclosure should be required to understand the problem, Hillary's role, the primary decision, the evidence supporting it, the current state, and the material limitation.

### 2. Depth is available without dominating

The complete blueprint, gallery, taxonomy, implementation detail, and secondary evidence remain present for interested reviewers.

### 3. Truth becomes easier to find

Status and ownership boundaries appear once in the strongest location and again only beside evidence whose meaning they change.

### 4. The hierarchy matches the job

Digital healthcare/product evidence leads. Delivery ownership remains prominent. Research-in-progress and client documentation are labeled as supporting evidence rather than treated as failed flagships.

### 5. Progressive disclosure is predictable

Use native disclosure behavior. Specific labels tell readers what they will inspect. Direct links open the correct disclosure without moving keyboard focus unexpectedly.

## Default recruiter path

1. Home establishes Healthcare Product Designer positioning and MSK proof.
2. Selected work prioritizes MSK, Grove, and Knowunity.
3. Medical Logistics appears as an always-visible Delivery Evidence proof.
4. UX Woman and Mobbin remain discoverable as supporting evidence.
5. Recruiter panel offers View résumé and Email me together.
6. Home Contact offers Email and View résumé plus the selectable email address.

## Case-by-case recut

### MSK

**Visible by default:** hero and implementation boundary; before/after workflow; one-button/four-rules decision; adoption handoff; qualified outcomes; independent proof.

**Optional depth:** complete service blueprint; two related workflow redesigns; detailed three-role history; system map and extended sustainment evidence.

**Disclosure labels:**

- `Inspect the complete service blueprint`
- `See the related MSK workflow redesigns`

### Grove

**Visible by default:** hero and phase status; five-person moderated test; 35-person survey and top three priorities; three product directions; calm-reminder/trust decision; next test.

**Optional depth:** complete first-build gallery; lower-ranked features; all five decision records; full component lab, tokens, principles, and implementation detail.

**Disclosure labels:**

- `Inspect Grove's complete research evidence`
- `Try the full Grove prototype system`

### Knowunity

**Visible by default:** hero and prototype status; brief; four interaction decisions; working prototype and Storybook actions; contribution split; result and required student test.

**Optional depth:** full constraint ledger; complete reel/transcript; route and component implementation details; extended AI-build disclosure.

**Disclosure label:**

- `Inspect all Knowunity implementation decisions`

### UX Woman

**Visible by default:** hero and research status; ownership problem; six-interview method summary; three strongest findings; next experiment; `0 of 5 run`.

**Optional depth:** full participant and secondary-research detail; all five findings; all five pretotype specifications.

**Disclosure label:**

- `Review all five findings and pretotypes`

### Medical Logistics

**Visible by default:** hero and scale; stakes; three service changes; qualified time, spending, and protocol outcomes.

**Optional depth:** complete constraint evidence; detailed mechanism diagrams and service explanation.

**Disclosure label:**

### Mobbin

**Visible by default:** hero and ownership boundary; screenshot-versus-flow distinction; one decision sequence; outputs.

**Optional depth:** full taxonomy/index artifact; complete three-app gallery; per-app analysis.

**Disclosure label:**

- `Explore the complete three-app capture archive`

## Content guardrails

- Use existing, source-backed copy and artifacts; no placeholder or invented evidence.
- Keep role, contribution, status, ownership boundaries, sample sizes, attribution, strongest artifact, primary decision, outcome/current state, and material limitations visible.
- Remove repeated disclaimers only after confirming the surviving statement remains adjacent to the claim it qualifies.
- Use plain, specific disclosure labels; never `Learn more`, `More`, or `Details` alone.
- Do not imply MSK implementation ownership, Grove launch, Knowunity testing/shipping, UX Woman validation, Mobbin product ownership, or unqualified Logistics causality.
- Do not call every project a flagship.

## Interaction requirements

- Reuse native `<details>` and `<summary>` with existing `.rp-deepDive` styling.
- The disclosure summary remains keyboard operable, screen-reader legible, and usable without JavaScript.
- When a URL hash targets an element inside a closed disclosure, open the nearest enclosing disclosure before the browser completes the arrival behavior.
- Preserve native history and Back behavior.
- Do not programmatically move focus on page load or hash changes.
- Preserve visible focus, 44px touch targets, reduced-motion behavior, and high-zoom/reflow access.

## Success criteria

The recut succeeds when an unfamiliar reviewer can identify, from the default path alone:

- the target role;
- the three primary digital cases;
- Medical Logistics as strong delivery ownership;
- each case's problem, role, evidence, decision, current state, and material limitation;
- a résumé action and an email action;
- where to inspect deeper evidence without losing their place.

It also succeeds when direct links, chapter navigation, keyboard use, mobile orientation, Spanish behavior, build checks, and existing public claims remain intact.

## Non-goals

- Creating new evidence or changing project truth.
- Claiming a senior generalist product level the work does not prove.
- Rebranding or replacing the Riso visual system.
- Introducing a new global reading mode or custom accordion system.
- Rewriting Spanish case-study content in this slice.
- Committing, deploying, or changing external job materials without separate authorization.
