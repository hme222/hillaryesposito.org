# Positioning

_Decided 2026-08-29, via `portfolio-positioning`. Read this before writing
any headline, summary, or case-study order — it's the answer those should
draft from, not a re-open question._

## The pattern (evidence, not opinion)

The recurring, load-bearing expertise across the resume, the AIRR memo, and
the AIRR application draft is **operator trust and override behavior in
high-stakes, regulated systems** — not "AI design" generically. The memo's
whole argument is that fielding a tool and getting people to use it
*correctly* are separate problems; the AIRR "expertise" answer names
"when operators override it, how edge cases decide whether a tool gets
used correctly or bypassed"; the named influence is Don Norman's "human
error is usually design error." 13 years Army + 6 years MSK back this with
real, specific proof, not adjacent experience.

A second, quieter thread: founder/builder credibility (Grove, Hera) — real,
but secondary to the first.

## The tension

- **Track A** — operator-trust/AI-adoption specialist for regulated,
  high-stakes systems. Audience: government, healthcare enterprise, internal
  tools. Voice: policy-literate, credentialed (clearance, MHA, Lean Six
  Sigma). Proof: MSK, Logistics.
- **Track B** — craft + founder credibility, ships products end to end.
  Audience: consumer/startup. Voice: the warm-editorial Riso register the
  portfolio is actually built in. Proof: Grove, Hera.

## Decision

**Track A leads. Track B is range, not the headline.**

The portfolio's primary claim is operator-trust/AI-adoption expertise in
regulated systems (MSK, Logistics as lead proof); Grove and the founder
story stay in the portfolio as evidence of range and shipping ability, not
as the first thing a visitor is told to care about.

## What this changes, concretely

- Homepage headline and hero should lead with the Track A claim, not a
  generic "product designer" framing.
- Case-study order/prominence: MSK and Logistics carry the primary weight;
  Grove supports as range.
- `case-study-interview` sessions should draft toward this framing —
  Grove's interview in particular should surface *range* evidence (shipped
  it solo, founder judgment) rather than trying to compete with MSK/Logistics
  on regulated-systems stakes it doesn't have.
- Existing homepage meta description ("Healthcare Product Designer with
  13+ years...") is directionally correct but doesn't yet name the
  operator-trust/adoption specificity — worth sharpening once case-study
  interviews give it real language to draw from, not before.
