# Design Brief: Higgsfield Innovation Reset

**Date:** 2026-08-11  
**Status:** **Approved by Hillary Esposito on 2026-08-11**

## Owner-approved private comparison amendment — 2026-08-12

The original panel assignment remains limited to two candidate public directions. Hillary subsequently requested a third concept derived from Frame of Intent for the private, no-credit comparison lab.

This amendment authorizes **C · Spatial Intent Playground** only as an unlinked, `noindex` diagnostic control. It is not a third public recommendation and does not reverse the rejection of Frame of Intent on the homepage. C may test embodied spatial intent, simulated local/cloud routing, distributed-system recovery, idempotent execution, and receipts only when those ideas are expressed through one simple production task rather than a named methodology, dashboard, infrastructure diagram, or technical explainer.

C cannot graduate to the homepage, a case study, Higgsfield generation, or deployment without a separate owner decision after the private comparison.

## Problem Statement

Previous Higgsfield directions changed the styling of evidence but not the portfolio experience. Static tableaus became posters, and Frame of Intent became a long method explainer with controls. Both made the production method more visible than Hillary's actual work.

The new problem is not “how can the portfolio mention Higgsfield?” It is: **how can Higgsfield create a memorable, high-craft experience that a recruiter feels immediately, while the portfolio work remains the protagonist and the AI assistance remains nearly invisible?**

## Users

- Time-boxed recruiters scanning the homepage in 10–30 seconds.
- Product, AI-product, and design leaders evaluating visual judgment, interaction craft, and truth boundaries.
- Visitors using touch, keyboard, screen readers, reduced motion, low-data connections, zoom, or narrow screens.
- Visitors who may distrust obvious AI spectacle or synthetic product evidence.

## Panel Assignment

Produce **exactly two materially different Higgsfield concepts**. They may not be palette or motion variants of one composition.

Each concept must define:

- the visitor experience in one sentence;
- the current Higgsfield techniques it uses and why those techniques matter;
- the homepage teaser behavior;
- one smaller, project-specific expression for MSK, Grove, and Mobbin;
- which authentic portfolio artifacts it requires;
- the generative/deterministic compositing boundary;
- mobile, reduced-motion, low-data, and no-media equivalents;
- replacement safety when Hillary swaps a source artifact;
- production steps, likely credit risk, and a low-cost proof test;
- the specific reason it is not a poster, film player, dashboard, or method explainer.

## Constraints

- No public Frame of Intent, Decision Frame, Impossible Evidence Table interface, or renamed version of those structures. The private C diagnostic exception above does not authorize public reuse.
- No named methodology on the homepage.
- No oversized explainer copy, workflow controls, sliders, endpoint buttons, filter menus, camera-permission gimmicks, or AI feature demos.
- No self-contained poster composition with headline, artifact, controls, and CTA trapped in one rectangle.
- No generated or altered product UI, workflow wording, metrics, ownership, provenance, or project status.
- Higgsfield may transform only source-free atmosphere or explicitly approved non-evidence imagery. Protected evidence must remain exact and deterministic.
- The experience must remain complete without motion or generated media.
- The homepage teaser must not delay the existing recruiter path or materially increase reading length.
- The work must feel innovative without looking obviously AI-generated.
- The existing Grove artifact stays in its case-study context unless Hillary later supplies a stronger replacement.

## Existing Design System

Use the current Riso/editorial portfolio as the host system. Preserve quiet authority, warm restraint, organic material inside a disciplined grid, strong typography, direct routes, and the existing mobile/accessibility behavior. The new craft may temporarily break the grid only when that break has a clear visual or narrative purpose.

## Taste Direction

Target: surprising, tactile, cinematic, precise, and authored.  
Avoid: instructional, self-important, over-labeled, poster-like, dashboard-like, synthetic, glossy AI luxury, or “look what the tool can do.”  
Quality test: the innovation should be understood first as Hillary's visual/product judgment; Higgsfield should be discoverable only in a secondary making-of note, if named at all.

## Success Criteria

- Hillary sees two genuinely different concepts worth comparing.
- At least one concept creates an immediate “how did she do that?” reaction without requiring instructions.
- A recruiter can still identify the selected project and open its case study within ten seconds.
- Each concept protects exact evidence and remains coherent when one artifact changes.
- Each concept has one inexpensive proof test before significant Higgsfield credit use.
- The eventual selected direction must reach the existing ≥95 craft gate and pass accessibility, recruiter comprehension, provenance, performance, and anti-AI review.

## Out of Scope

- Implementing either concept before owner selection.
- New paid Higgsfield generations before a proof-test plan is approved.
- Camera/hand tracking, avatars, voice cloning, talking heads, or synthetic portraits.
- Rewriting the case studies or fabricating stronger artifacts.
- Deployment.
